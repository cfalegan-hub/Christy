Tax planning — general reliefs

Reliefs and allowances worth checking

General reference only — not personalised advice. None of this has been matched to real income or a confirmed business structure yet (open questions 2 and 6). Rates and thresholds change most tax years — confirm current figures with HMRC or a qualified accountant before relying on any of them.

Personal Allowance

The amount of income you don't pay Income Tax on each tax year. Applies automatically via PAYE or Self Assessment.

Marriage Allowance

Lets a lower-earning spouse or civil partner transfer part of their unused Personal Allowance to the other.

Depends on marital status — not on file

Trading Allowance

A small amount of self-employment or casual income can be earned tax-free without needing to register, if kept under the threshold.

Depends on business structure — open question 2

Property Allowance

The equivalent of the Trading Allowance for small amounts of property or rental income.

Depends on income sources — open question 6

ISA allowance

A tax-free wrapper for savings and investments. The allowance resets every tax year and doesn't carry over.

Pension Annual Allowance

Pension contributions receive tax relief up to an annual cap, which can also reduce adjusted net income for other thresholds.

Capital Gains Tax exempt amount

A tax-free allowance on gains from selling assets such as shares or a second property, before CGT applies.

Rent-a-Room Relief

Tax-free income from letting furnished accommodation in your own home, up to a set threshold, without needing to register as a landlord.

Gift Aid

Charitable donations made under Gift Aid can extend your basic-rate band and reduce higher-rate tax exposure.

Allowable business expenses

If self-employed, costs incurred wholly and exclusively for the business reduce taxable profit. Cash-basis simplified expenses may apply to things like mileage and use of home.

Depends on business structure — open question 2
